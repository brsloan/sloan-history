Sources & Loose Ends

Everything on this site comes out of the research folder this website lives in — about 900 files that Ben gathered between 2017 and 2019 — and has kept adding to on Ancestry since — from the Frankfort library’s scrapbooks, the courthouse, Ancestry, Newspapers.com, the South Carolina state archives, the Carroll County Historical Society, and cousins who answered their email. This page says who to thank, what is still unproven, and where to find every original.

  • Jill Sloan Walters (daughter of John E. Sloan; Uly’s granddaughter) had worked on the Sloans for years before Ben started. She supplied the 1936–1955 photographs from her mother’s albums, the wash kettle, and the memories of the farm and sawmill.
  • Jerilyn “Jeri” Hutchins (Aunt Ruth’s daughter) collected the Gum and Sharp photographs and wrote down Ruth’s memories in a series of emails with Dad in 2000.

Handle with careWhat isn’t proven

  • That our John Sloan (1793–1854) was a son of the old soldier John Sloan of Laurens. Strong circumstantial case; no document. Pages of the Ora A.R.P. church records obtained since name the old soldier’s other children, but not, so far, John and Margaret. (Margaret’s parentage — Archibald Sloan and Frances Drennan — is proven, by their Newberry estate files.)
  • The old soldier’s age of 113, and most of the colorful detail about him. See A Soldier of ’76.
  • That Thomas Pickering was the son of the transported convict Gawan Pickering. Plausible, flagged “assume” even by the Pickering researchers who proposed it.
  • Exactly when the Sloans reached Indiana. After November 1827, before about 1831.
  • What became of Elisabeth C. Sloan, the first of the family born in Indiana. Ben’s notes chase her to a James Thompson, possibly to Kansas.
  • John Simmons’s parents (Grandma Minnie’s grandfather), Mahlon Sharp’s real surname and village, and any Irish townland for the Lowell families.
  • John and Margaret’s gravestones at Old Providence — ordered and paid for in 1855, and not to be found today.
  • The Sloan family Bible, said to have come down through Lizzie Sloan Person’s family (the Bryans of Mulberry); a relative’s genealogy notes on that side are said to have burned in a house fire.
  • Bessie’s death date: her obituary implies Monday 6 September 1954, her stone says 5 September, and Ellis’s obituary says the 4th.
  • The Sanky Bennett stories on the sawmill page are family memory; nobody has yet looked for him in prison or census records.
  • Which Samuel McQuerns did what. The tree has a father (1765–1820) and a son (1789–1852) of the same name. The father must be the witness to Robert Drennan’s will in 1801 and the sailor of the Royal George story; the son is the Indiana settler. An earlier version of these pages ran the two together.
  • James J. Sloan (1798–1860) as a son of Archibald and Fanny: claimed by a book on Newberry land grants, doubted by Ben.
  • The Torrence line now runs, on paper, to Adam Torrence Sr., killed at Ramsour’s Mill in 1780: his son’s pension file, his widow Ann’s estate division, and the 1844 will of the son tie the generations together. Adam Sr.’s own trail runs back through a 1765 Rowan County deed and three 1762 Pennsylvania land warrants on the Juniata frontier. The step above that, to Hugh Torrance of Londonderry and Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, rests only on a 1938 book and is contradicted by Hugh’s own will and by where the two men’s land lay. George’s own death (after 1902) and Mollie’s maiden name (“Grier” rests on one Iowa death record) are not settled, and Isabella’s surname is unknown. The Hugh Torrance of 1732 that an earlier version of this site claimed belonged to a different Ralph. Ralph Richardson Torrence’s Greenville obituary also calls him “a native of North Dakota” — almost certainly a misprint for North Carolina.
  • William K. Compton’s 1872 “hospital inquest.” The record is in the state archives; whether it committed him to the asylum at Indianapolis, where he died the next year, has not been read.
  • Two sets of Comptons: Martha Sloan’s husband John Compton of Laurens, South Carolina, and Jane Sloan’s husband William K. Compton of Indiana. The same family, or a coincidence of names?
